**In one sentence:** multinational buyers commonly ask whether a supplier has a way for people to raise concerns confidentially, and a smaller company can answer with a simple channel that it actually runs.

## Why buyers ask

A buyer cannot watch its suppliers every day. A reporting channel is one of the few ways for a problem inside a supplier, such as bribery, fraud or harassment, to come to light. So it appears in supplier questionnaires and audits, next to the code of conduct and the training records.

Buyers also read the channel as a signal. A supplier that has one, explains it and responds to it looks more careful than one that does not.

## What a credible channel has

- **Easy to find.** Employees and partners should know where it is without asking.
- **Confidential, with an anonymous option.** A good default is that reports are anonymous, and that the person may choose to leave contact details.
- **Categories.** Corruption or bribery, conflict of interest, fraud, harassment and other.
- **A way to follow up.** A tracking code lets the person check the status without identifying themselves.
- **A commitment to review.** Each report gets looked at, and what happens is recorded.

## A channel is a process, not a form

The form is the easy part. Decide in advance who reads the reports, who can see them, and what happens next. Decide what to do when a report concerns the person who runs the channel. Think about how long you keep reports and who may access them. These choices affect the people who report, so they deserve a lawyer's review, especially where personal data rules apply.

## Anonymity has limits

Be careful about what you promise. An anonymous channel in your system does not mean that every network or service between the reporter and you records nothing. Describe what your channel does and does not do, and avoid broad promises.

## Show that it is used

Buyers like to see more than the existence of the channel. A short note on how people are told about it, how reports are handled and how the records are kept is often more convincing than a long policy. Training that mentions the channel, and acknowledgments that confirm people were told, help tie it together.

## Tell people, then remind them

Most channels fail quietly because people forget they exist. Mention the channel in onboarding, in training and in the code of conduct, and remind people from time to time. Make clear that raising a concern in good faith is welcome. Tell partners and vendors too, since buyers often expect the channel to be open to them as well.

## What to do next

1. Choose who receives and reviews reports.
2. Publish a simple channel and tell your people and partners where it is.
3. Write down in a few lines how reports are handled.
4. Record each report and the follow-up.
5. Ask a lawyer to review confidentiality, retention and personal data.
